# Script: How DNA packs into a chromosome

Spoken words: 126 (answer and seam lines included). Speech: 126 ÷ 2.6 ≈ 48 s.
Estimated total: 48 + 0.7 × 9 lines + 3 beats + about 6 s of kit overhead (no cards) + about 2 s of moves ≈ 66 s.

    OPENING  [frame 0: a double helix across the frame, turning about its axis; no card]
    Each of your cells holds about two metres of DNA, in a nucleus six thousandths of a
    millimetre wide.  [`2 m` then `6 µm` fade in under the helix as each is said] (beat)
    [title as type over the helix; the two numbers leave with it]
    So how does two metres of DNA pack into tiny chromosomes? Wind it, then loop it.

    1 Wind it  [no card; the helix stays]
    First, wind it.
    [the view pulls back: the helix shrinks away, eight orange spheres appear, the thread wraps
    1.67 times round them; `1.67 turns`, then `8 histones`; the unit turns]
    DNA's twisted ladder, the double helix, wraps almost twice around a spool of eight proteins
    called histones.
    [`nucleosome` under the unit; it shrinks into a row of seven beads on one thread, turning;
    caption `beads on a string · about 10 nm thick; the bare helix is 2 nm`]
    Each wrapped spool is a nucleosome. Spool after spool, the DNA becomes beads on a string. (beat)

    [bridge, over the turning beads; nothing new drawn]
    But that string is still far too long.

    2 Loop it  [no card; the string whips off to the left along its own length]
    [the string again, standing upright; a pink scaffold, `condensins`; loops grow out from it all round, `loops`; the array turns]
    So before a cell divides, proteins called condensins pull it into loops, packed around a
    central scaffold.
    [the loop array shrinks into an X-shaped chromosome; `≈10,000×`; the chromosome turns once
    while the camera tilts; `chromosome · one of 46 in a human cell`]
    Loop upon loop, the DNA becomes about ten thousand times shorter. That is a chromosome. (beat)

    ENDING  [camera levels; the chromosome moves down; the question returns above it]
    Wound on histone spools, then looped: about ten thousand times shorter.
    To go deeper, read about chromatin in OpenStax Biology.
