# How DNA packs into a chromosome — facts

Every claim the video speaks or shows is a line here with its source. The video's references are its
sources, so lines are `[SOURCE]`. Quotes are in `research/notes.md`.

Short names: WP = en.wikipedia.org/wiki, OS = https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/14-2-dna-structure-and-sequencing

## The problem

- [SOURCE] Each human cell holds roughly two metres of DNA. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus (genome.gov gives "5 to 6 feet": https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Chromatin)
- [SOURCE] A human cell's nucleus is about six micrometres across (six thousandths of a millimetre). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_nucleus
- [SOURCE] A human cell has 46 chromosomes. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome

## The double helix

- [SOURCE] DNA is two strands coiled around each other, a double helix. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA
- [SOURCE] The double helix is 2 nm in diameter. — OS

## Winding: the nucleosome

- [SOURCE] DNA wraps around proteins called histones to form nucleosomes. — OS
- [SOURCE] The histone core is eight proteins (two each of H2A, H2B, H3, H4). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleosome
- [SOURCE] About 147 base pairs wrap 1.67 turns ("almost twice") around the core. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin (147 bp), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleosome (146 bp, 1.67 turns)
- [SOURCE] Repeating nucleosomes joined by linker DNA form a fibre called "beads on a string", about 10 nm thick. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleosome, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin (10–11 nm)
- [SOURCE] Beads on a string shorten DNA only about five to ten times, far short of what is needed ("still far too long"). — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleosome

## Looping: the chromosome

- [SOURCE] The beads-on-a-string fibre forms loops. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin
- [SOURCE] Condensins are protein complexes central to chromosome condensation; condensin extrudes and enlarges DNA loops. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condensin
- [SOURCE] In the condensed chromosome the loops are held on a central scaffold of proteins, which includes condensin. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome
- [SOURCE] Chromatin condenses into visible chromosomes during cell division. — https://www.genome.gov/genetics-glossary/Chromatin, OS
- [SOURCE] In the condensed chromosome the DNA is about ten-thousand-fold shorter. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome
- [SOURCE] A condensed chromosome is a pair of sister chromatids joined at the centromere, X-shaped when the centromere is near the middle. — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome
