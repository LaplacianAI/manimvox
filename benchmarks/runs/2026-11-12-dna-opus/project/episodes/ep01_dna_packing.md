# ep01 How DNA fits in a chromosome — script

Spoken words: 137 in the final .srt (answer, bridge and READ line included), 10 lines; speech 47.6 s at 173 wpm; final length 65.5 s.

OPENING  [cold open: a thread runs edge to edge; "≈ 2 m of DNA" in accent; a small circle, "nucleus 10–20 µm"; note "not to scale"]
Each human cell holds about two metres of DNA, in a nucleus just ten to twenty microns across.
[intro card=False: title type over the scene; question "How does two metres of DNA pack into chromosomes?"]
So how does that thread pack into chromosomes? In two moves: spools, then loops.

01 Spools  [seam: zoom_through(nucleus), no card]
[3D double helix along x, the camera tilts; "2 nm" with a width bar]
Up close, the thread is a double helix, two nanometres wide.
[an orange spool of 8 spheres appears; the helix as a tube wraps ~1.7 turns round it; "8 histone proteins", "≈ 147 base pairs"]
It wraps almost twice around a spool of eight histone proteins. Spool and DNA together make a nucleosome.
[a row of nucleosomes on a strand, beads on a string; "≈ 7× shorter"]
Repeated along the strand like beads on a string, nucleosomes make it about seven times shorter. (beat)
[bridge, over the beads]
But that is still far too long.

02 Loops  [seam: "dip", no card]
[3D vertical axis; violet loops fan out from it one by one; yellow-green rings at their bases; "condensin"]
Before a cell divides, protein rings called condensin pull the string into loops along a central axis.
[loops fill the axis into a thick rod; the camera orbits; the rod and its copy join at the centromere: "sister chromatids"]
Stacked loops thicken the axis into a chromosome: two copies, joined in the middle. (beat)

ENDING  [clear blur; the thread and nucleus again; the thread collapses into a small chromosome inside the circle; "≈ 10,000× shorter"]
[outro(answer=…), keep=[nucleus, chromosome]]
Answer: Spools, then loops: about ten thousand times shorter than the bare thread.
Next: to read more, start with Nature Scitable on DNA packaging.
