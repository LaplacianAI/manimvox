## The length problem
- [SOURCE] A diploid human cell contains about 2 metres of DNA. — S1
- [SOURCE] The nucleus is typically only 10 to 20 microns in diameter. — S1

## The helix
- [SOURCE] B-form DNA, the main form in cells, is about 2 nm wide. — S5

## Spools: the nucleosome
- [SOURCE] A histone octamer is two copies each of H2A, H2B, H3 and H4: eight histone proteins. — S1, S2
- [SOURCE] About 147 base pairs of DNA wrap around the octamer to form the nucleosome core particle (S2: 146 or 147 observed; S1: about 146). — S3, S2
- [SOURCE] The DNA wraps about 1.7 turns (1.67 left-handed superhelical turns) around the octamer: "almost twice". — S1, S2
- [SOURCE] Nucleosomes joined by linker DNA form a "beads-on-a-string" fibre about 10–11 nm across. — S3, S1
- [SOURCE] Nucleosome packaging shortens the fibre about sevenfold. — S1

## Loops: the chromosome
- [SOURCE] In mitotic chromosomes, condensin forms loops by loop extrusion; condensin II makes longer loops and sets up the chromosome's axis, condensin I shorter loops that thicken it. — S4
- [SOURCE] Condensin is drawn as a ring. — S3 (figure: "condensin rings")
- [SOURCE] The packing ratio of DNA in metaphase chromosomes is about 10,000 to 1. — S1
- [SOURCE] A replicated chromosome is two identical sister chromatids joined at the centromere. — S6

## Left out (not claimed)
- [OPEN] Whether a regular 30 nm fibre exists in living cells is debated; the video does not draw one. — S3
