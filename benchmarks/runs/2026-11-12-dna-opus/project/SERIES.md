# How DNA fits in a chromosome — outline

## The question

Question: How does two metres of DNA pack into chromosomes?

Situation that shows it: each human cell holds about two metres of DNA, and its nucleus is only ten to twenty microns across.

## The viewer

A general viewer with school biology: knows DNA is a double helix and cells have a nucleus; has never heard of histones, nucleosomes or condensin.

## The theme

Dark-field microscope look, set in series.json → theme. Fixed colours: DNA strands STRAND_A (blue) and STRAND_B (teal), histones HISTONE (orange), loops LOOP (violet), condensin CONDENSIN (yellow-green), numbers ACCENT.

## Shape (about one minute, 2 chapters)

| # | Idea | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| open | the length cannot fit | a thread edge to edge, "≈ 2 m of DNA"; a small nucleus circle, "10–20 µm"; "not to scale" |
| 1 Spools | the helix wraps around histone spools: nucleosomes, beads on a string, ~7× shorter | 3D helix (2 nm), the camera tilts; an orange spool of 8 histone spheres; a DNA tube wraps ~1.7 turns; a row of beads on a string, "≈ 7× shorter" |
| 2 Loops | condensin pulls the string into loops along an axis; stacked loops make a chromosome | 3D: violet loops fan out round a vertical axis, condensin rings at their bases; the camera orbits; the rod becomes two sister chromatids joined at the centromere |
| end | answer | back to the thread and nucleus; the thread collapses to a small chromosome, "≈ 10,000× shorter" |

Exit: spools, then loops, about ten thousand times shorter. Next: read Nature Scitable on DNA packaging.

Cut for length: the 30 nm fibre (debated in living cells), linker DNA lengths, histone H1, interphase loops and cohesin, the two condensins' separate roles.

## Running example

The same thread from the opening, followed through every stage.
