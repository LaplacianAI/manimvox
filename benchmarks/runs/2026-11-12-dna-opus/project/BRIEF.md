# How DNA fits in a chromosome: brief

What the person wants from this video, asked before any outline. The outline, the script, the opening, the ending and the theme follow from it. An answer the person left open is decided by the agent and marked *(chosen by the agent)*.

## Who it is for

> Who is this video for, and what do they already know? It decides the words used, which terms are defined, and how fast it moves.

A general audience with school biology: they know DNA is a double helix and that cells have a nucleus, but not how it is packed. (chosen by the agent: the request names no audience, and 'explain how' suggests newcomers)

## What they take away

> What should they understand, or be able to do, when it ends? It decides the one question the video opens on and answers at the end.

That a chromosome is DNA wound and folded in stages: the helix wraps around histone spools, the beads fold into loops, and the loops gather around an axis, about ten thousand times shorter. (chosen by the agent: this is the packing the request asks about)

## How it should feel

> How should it feel to watch? It decides the pace, the mood of the narration, and how much is shown at once.

Curious, a puzzle being solved: a length that cannot fit, then made to fit, stage by stage. (chosen by the agent)

## Your angle

> Why this topic for you? Is there a story, example or way of explaining it that you want in? It decides the running example, the story the chapters follow, the personal touch.

Zoom out level by level, from one helix to one chromosome, with the length shrinking at each stage. (chosen by the agent: the request asks for 3D graphics, and depth is the idea at each level)

## How it opens

> How should it open? It decides the first 10 to 20 seconds, which decide whether people keep watching.

## How it ends

> How should it end? It decides the last 10 to 20 seconds, what people remember.

Answer the opening, calling back to its scene: the same two-metre thread, now a chromosome. (chosen by the agent)
