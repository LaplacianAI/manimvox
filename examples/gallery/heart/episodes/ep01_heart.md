# Inside the Heart (about 90 seconds)

Question: What is inside the heart, and how does blood move through it?

## Opening
- Your heart is two pumps in one. Let's open one up and see.

## 1 The parts  (picture: a real CT heart turns once; its front is sliced away to the four-chamber view; labels with leader lines)
- This is a real human heart, rebuilt from a CT scan.
- Slice away the front, and four chambers appear.
- On top, the two atria take blood in.
- Below, the two ventricles pump it out.
- A wall of tissue, the septum, keeps the two sides apart.
- And valves, in yellow, keep blood moving one way.

## 2 Two pumps  (picture: blue blood streams through the right side, red through the left; dashed exits for arteries in front of the cut)
- Oxygen-poor blood from the body enters the right atrium.
- It passes the tricuspid valve into the right ventricle,
- which pumps it through the pulmonary valve, to the lungs.
- Oxygen-rich blood comes back from the lungs into the left atrium.
- It passes the mitral valve into the left ventricle,
- which pumps it through the aortic valve, to the whole body.
- Right side to the lungs, left side to the body: two pumps, side by side.

## Ending
- Recap: atria take in, ventricles pump out; septum and valves; right to lungs, left to body.
- Answer: four chambers and their valves; body, right side, lungs, left side, body.
