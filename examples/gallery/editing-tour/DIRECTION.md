# Your footage, edited: direction

## Three storylines

### A. The repair bench (recommended)

- **Device:** a repair. Four raw recordings arrive broken: an interview with pauses and an "um", a demolition filmed by a shaking hand, a shoot in front of a green sheet, a street scene from two cameras. Each chapter fixes one kind of problem, and every feature is a tool on the bench.
- **Form:** a split bench. On the left, the raw clip as it is; on the right, the edit file, with the line being explained lit in the accent. The result then plays full frame. A before and after sits side by side where the change is easier to see that way. Each feature ends on its common mistake, drawn as the wrong line struck through and the right one under it.
- **First 2 seconds:** frame 0 is the four raw clips in a 2×2 grid, each showing its problem: the speaker stopped mid-pause, the towers jumping, the green sheet, the two cameras with their sound waves out of line. The shaking clip is already shaking.
- **Last shot:** the whole edit file beside the finished film's last frame, every line lit.

### B. One file, line by line

- **Device:** reading a recipe. The edit file is read from its first line to its last; each line is one feature.
- **Form:** the file is always on screen on the left, the picture on the right; each chapter zooms into the next line, and the footage changes as the line is typed.
- **First 2 seconds:** an empty edit file with a cursor, and `{"segments": [` typing in.
- **Last shot:** the whole file, scrolled, beside the film.

### C. Four films

- **Device:** four short films, one per recording, each using the features its footage needs.
- **Form:** each film opens on its raw footage as the chapter title; features are taught inside the film that needs them.
- **First 2 seconds:** the interview, frozen mid-"um", with the waveform under it.
- **Last shot:** the four finished films in a 2×2 grid.

**Recommended: A, the repair bench,** because the brief opens on the raw footage's problems and ends on the edit file, and a bench keeps the before, the field and the after in one place for a viewer who has never written an edit file. B hides the footage behind the file for too long; C repeats features across films, or teaches them out of order.

**Picked: A, the repair bench** (by the person).

## Direction

**Look from one medium: the editor's bench.** A dark desk under real footage.
- Ground: `bg` #0E1822, solid (footage carries the texture).
- Ink: #E6EEF2 for text, #93A7B4 muted; panels #16242F.
- One accent, teal #4FC3C9, only on the thing the narration is naming: the lit line of the edit file, the ring on the footage.
- Mistakes in #EF6F7E (struck through), the fix in #7CC8A0 under it. Nowhere else.
- Sizes: chapter title 54 pt, body 30 pt, labels 24 pt, code 26 pt mono.

**Motion by role.** Enter 0.9 to 1.1 s, move 1.0 s, exit 0.5 s. No more than 3 things moving at once. A title holds still once it lands.

**The bench layout.** Raw clip on the left, 55% of the width; the edit file panel on the right, its lines dim except the one being explained. The result plays full frame from the edit itself, never as a small window. Before and after: two equal halves, labelled once.

**Opening shot.** The four raw clips in a 2×2 grid, the shaking one moving, each with its problem labelled under it.

**Ending.** The finished edit file, scrolled, beside the film; each part lights as its piece plays, ending on the whole file lit.

**Sound.** The voice is always the loudest thing. Footage under narration sits 18 dB down or muted; footage comes up only when it is the point (the interview speaking, the crash, the sound chapter's own demo). Music bed at -26 dB, dipping further under speech.

**Seams.** Chapters change by what is on screen: the lit line of the edit file zooms to become the next chapter's field; a cut on the footage's own action (the towers falling, a camera switch); a dip between the 0.8.0 and 0.8.5 halves. At least 3 kinds, never the same at every seam.

**BANNED.**
- the same title card every chapter, a faded chapter number
- everything fading and sliding up
- a recap bullet slide
- narration reading the code panel aloud character by character (say what the field means)
- footage louder than the voice
- a small floating window for the result (results play full frame)
- a heading on every scene
