# An interview, edited: brief

## Who it is for

People who have their own footage (an interview, a talk, a screen recording) and want to know what `manimvox edit` does with it. They know nothing about the speaker's field. (chosen by the agent)

## What they take away

A real, unscripted interview becomes a tight, captioned film from a text file: cut by the speaker's own words, with pauses and "um"s gone, her name and role on screen, and a narrated Manim diagram where a picture explains better than talk. (chosen by the agent)

## How it should feel

Like a short, well-made news piece: calm, nothing showy, the speaker always the centre. (chosen by the agent)

## Your angle

Real footage, not a staged demo: a 2013 interview with Prof Joyeeta Gupta from the UNESCO-IHE archive (CC BY 3.0), unedited and with its natural pauses. Two of her answers, each after its question on a card, and the plan she lists drawn as a diagram between them. (chosen by the agent)

## How it opens

A title card naming the speaker and where it was recorded, then straight into her introducing herself. (chosen by the agent)

## How it ends

Her last point lands, then an end card with the credit for the footage and how it was edited. (chosen by the agent)

## Tone of voice

Her own words carry it. The one narrated part, the diagram, is plain and short, and says only what she said. (chosen by the agent)

## The look

The footage full frame. Cards and the diagram on a calm deep-blue ground with a water-teal accent, thin strokes. Captions at the bottom, a lower third once for her name. (chosen by the agent)

## Length and pace

About 75 seconds. (chosen by the agent)

## Voice

kokoro:af_heart for the diagram, offline. (chosen by the agent)
