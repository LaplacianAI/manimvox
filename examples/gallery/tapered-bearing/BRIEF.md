# A bearing, opened: brief

What the person wants from this video. Nobody could be asked, so every answer is decided by the agent and marked *(chosen by the agent)*.

## Who it is for

> Who is this video for, and what do they already know? It decides the words used, which terms are defined, and how fast it moves.

A curious non-engineer: someone who has heard "the wheel bearing is gone" about a bike or a car and nodded without knowing what a bearing is. They know what a wheel and an axle are. *(chosen by the agent)*

## What they take away

> What should they understand, or be able to do, when it ends? It decides the one question the video opens on and answers at the end.

How a heavy wheel turns freely: a ring of rollers sits between the fixed axle and the turning wheel, the rollers roll instead of sliding, and because they are slightly cone-shaped and set on a slant they also hold the sideways push of a turn. The question: "What lets a loaded wheel turn so freely?" *(chosen by the agent)*

## How it should feel

> How should it feel to watch? It decides the pace, the mood of the narration, and how much is shown at once.

Calm and clear, like a good teacher at a workbench: one part on screen, one thing named at a time. *(chosen by the agent)*

## Your angle

> Why this topic for you? Is there a story, example or way of explaining it that you want in? It decides the running example, the story the chapters follow, the personal touch.

One running example, the wheel of a car. The bearing is taken out of the hub, cut open a quarter at a time the way an engineer's drawing does, and put back at the end. The model is a real open CAD part, so the video says where it comes from. *(chosen by the agent)*

## How it opens

> How should it open? It decides the first 10 to 20 seconds, which decide whether people keep watching.

A scene that shows the problem happening: a loaded wheel turning, with the weight pressing on its axle, and the question. No title card. *(chosen by the agent)*

## How it ends

> How should it end? It decides the last 10 to 20 seconds, what people remember.

Back to the wheel from the opening, now with the bearing in place, and the answer to the opening question beside it. No recap bullets. *(chosen by the agent)*

## Tone of voice

> What tone should the narration have? It decides the wording of every line.

Plain spoken English, short sentences, no jargon without a phrase of explanation. *(chosen by the agent)*
