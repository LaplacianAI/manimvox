# Script: A bearing, opened

Storyline A (`DIRECTION.md`). Spoken words: about 150. Rendered: 60 s. Facts: `FACTS.md`, "Tapered roller bearings".

OPENING [cold open: a wheel turning on its axle, the weight arrow pressing on the axle (accent)]
A car wheel carries the weight of the whole car, and still it turns almost freely.
[intro, card=False: the question types in over the wheel]
What lets a loaded wheel turn so freely? A ring of rollers, set at a slant.

01 Inside the hub [cardless seam, zoom through the ring on the axle; narration over the seam]
Between the axle and the wheel sits a bearing.
[the bearing, face on, swings up to three quarters, then turns once on its own axis; credit line small]
Here it is, taken out of the hub.
[a quarter wedge is cut away, the cut faces show the rings and a roller]
Cut a quarter away, and the inside shows.
[labels grow as named; the named part takes the accent: inner ring, outer ring, rollers]
An inner ring, on the axle. An outer ring, in the wheel. Between them, rollers. (beat)

02 The load [cardless seam; the camera pushes in on one roller]
Now the weight.
[radial arrow through the bore towards the roller, labelled "weight"]
The car's weight pushes down on the axle. The rollers pass it to the outer ring, and they roll, so nothing drags.
[axial arrow along the axle, labelled "sideways push"]
A turn pushes sideways as well.

03 Why tapered [cardless seam; flat drawing, marked "simplified"]
So why are the rollers tapered?
[one roller between two rings, drawn at a slant; the ring pushes on it at right angles to the slant; the push splits into "weight" down and "sideways" along]
Each roller is a slice of a cone, set on a slant, so one roller takes the weight and the sideways push together.
[dashed lines from the rollers and rings meet at one point on the axle line]
Extend their lines, and they all meet at one point on the axle. So they roll, not slide. (beat)

ENDING [the wheel from the opening, bearing ring lit, the answer above it]
Cone-shaped rollers between two rings: they roll instead of sliding, and their slant carries the weight and the sideways push.

## Critique
1. A problem in 20 s? yes: a loaded wheel, the question.
2. One question, answered in the same words? yes ("What lets a loaded wheel turn so freely?" / the answer in `answer=`).
3. Promise and route? the opening names the answer's parts (rollers, slant).
4. Each term defined once, after the need? bearing (on the axle), inner/outer ring and rollers named as the part is lit; taper defined as "a slice of a cone".
5. Chapters open on something concrete, end on a takeaway with a beat? yes (ch.1 and ch.3 beat; ch.2 ends on the sideways push that ch.3 answers).
6. Seams are bridges? "Now the weight." / "So why are the rollers tapered?" yes.
7. Running example in every chapter? the wheel's weight, in 2 and 3, and the ending.
8. Ending solves the opening on screen? yes, same wheel.
9. Recap? none (brief).
10. Next? none; a single video, the answer is the ending.
11. Length? about 70 s with overhead, a little over the minute asked; the cut: the cage and the history.
12. Every claim in FACTS.md? yes; the drawing is marked "simplified".
13. Numbers true on screen? no numbers; the drawing is labelled simplified.
14. Thread? The sideways push is introduced in ch.2 and explained in ch.3.

The outro adds "That is the whole trick." after the answer.
