# Which site am I on?: brief

## Who it is for

Anyone who uses a browser every day but has never been told what the strip at the top of the window is for. No technical background. (chosen by the agent)

## What they take away

The address bar belongs to the browser, not the page: its domain says which site you are on, and the shield beside it says the connection is encrypted. Read those two, not the page's own title. (chosen by the agent)

## How it should feel

Calm and a little reassuring: "oh, that is what that is". (chosen by the agent)

## Your angle

A real screenshot of a real browser (Firefox 157 showing Wikipedia, from Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 4.0), shown large, with its parts named one at a time as the voice reaches them. No made-up interface. (chosen by the agent)

## How it opens

Straight in, no title card: the browser window already on screen and the question "Which part of this window tells you which site you are really on?" (chosen by the agent)

## How it ends

Answer the opening question over the same window, with the address bar lit and the credit line still showing. (chosen by the agent)

## Tone of voice

Plain spoken English, short sentences, no hype. (chosen by the agent)

## The look

Light paper ground, ink text, one blue accent used only on the part being named; calm style; no header label or progress bar. (chosen by the agent)

## Length and pace

About 40 seconds, unhurried. (chosen by the agent)
